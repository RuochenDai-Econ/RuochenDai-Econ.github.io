Community Networks and Entrepreneurship in a Developing Economy

with Dilip Mookherjee, Kaivan Munshi and Xiaobo Zhang

Paper | BibTeX

Abstract

We study the role played by community-based business networks in China’s transition from agriculture to domestic production in the 1990’s and the subsequent transition to exporting in the 2000’s. The model of occupational mobility that we develop and test incorporates a productivity enhancing role for these networks. Using administrative data covering the universe of registered firms over the 1994-2012 period and the universe of exporters over the 2002-2012 period, we provide causal evidence that networks of firms organized around the rural birth county increased the revenues of their members, both in domestic production and exporting. While this substantially increased entry into domestic production in the first transition, the incumbent domestic networks created a disincentive to enter exporting in the second transition that outweighed the positive effect of newly emerging export networks, highlighting the contrasting roles that the same networks can play at different stages of economic development.

Export Destinations, Quality, and Automation: Evidence from China

with Kailin Gao and Rui Zhang

Paper | BibTeX

Abstract

Automation technology not only saves labor costs but also facilitates quality upgrading, so growing export demands in high-income destinations with stronger quality preference may induce exporters to automate more actively. We illustrate this insight in a model with non-homethetic preference and comparative advantage in quality production generated by automation technology. We test the key model prediction using Chinese firm-level trade data from 2001 to 2015, and find that increased demand from high-income destinations causes rising firm-level imports of automation machines, while increased demand from low-income destinations does not. This effect is driven by the imports of regulating and control instruments, a type of automation machines crucial for quality control and monitoring, and by imports sourced from high-income economies. Our finding highlights the importance of access to high-income destinations for promoting technology adoption in developing economies.
